# Cold Outreach Kit — Practice Revenue Blueprint

You don't have warm contacts to lean on, so this kit is built for cold outreach to practices that
have never heard of you. The good news: your offer is unusually strong for cold work, because three
things do the persuading for you —

1. **A real, specific news hook:** Medicare changed how it pays for care management in 2025. True, recent, and most practices genuinely don't know.
2. **A free proof piece:** the Westbrook sample Blueprint. You're not asking them to imagine it — you show them.
3. **Zero-risk guarantee:** $50k or they don't pay. This removes the reason to say no.

Your job on a cold call is **not** to sell the $1,500 engagement. It's to earn one of two small yeses:
**(a) permission to email the sample**, or **(b) a 15-minute intake call.** That's it.

> **Compliance note:** these are B2B calls to business phone lines, which is normal practice.
> Be professional, identify yourself and your company, and honor any "remove me" request.
> Moonlighting guardrail still applies: do not contact any practice you serve through the ADA grant.

---

## Who to target (the ideal cold prospect)
- **Independent primary care practices** — Family Medicine or Internal Medicine.
- **1–6 physicians.** Big enough to have real Medicare volume, small enough that the owner/manager makes the decision quickly.
- **NOT** large hospital-owned groups or FQHCs first (long procurement, no fast yes).
- **Higher Medicare mix** is better (rural, suburban, older patient base).
- Decision maker = the **physician-owner** or the **practice/office manager**. The front desk is the gatekeeper.

## How to find them (free)
- **NPI Registry** (npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov) — search by taxonomy "Family Medicine" / "Internal Medicine" + city/state. Free, public, gives practice name, address, phone. *(Ask me — I can pull you a starter list of real practices in any metro you name.)*
- Google Maps "family medicine [town]" — independent practices, phone numbers, often the owner's name.
- Your state medical society / county IPA directories.
- Build a simple sheet: Practice · City · Phone · Contact name (if known) · Status · Next action.

---

## The sequence (per prospect)
1. **Call.** Aim for permission to email the sample (or book the intake).
2. **No answer →** leave the 15-second voicemail **and** send the cold email same day (reference the voicemail).
3. **Sample sent →** follow up by phone 3 business days later to book the intake.
4. **Stop after 3 touches** if no response. Move on; volume wins.

Target: ~15–20 dials/hour. Expect most to not pick up — that's normal. A handful of conversations a day is the goal.

## COLD CALL SCRIPT

### Getting past the front desk (gatekeeper)
> "Hi, this is **Denise Campbell** with **Campbell3**. I help primary care practices with the **Medicare
> care-management billing changes** that took effect in 2025. Who's the best person there to talk to
> about the practice's Medicare revenue — **Dr. [Last Name]** or your **practice manager**?"

- If asked "what's this regarding?": *"Medicare changed how it pays practices for managing chronic patients in 2025 — most practices are leaving real money uncaptured. I have a free one-page example I'd like to get to the right person."*
- If "send an email": *"Happy to — what's the best address, and who should I address it to?"* (Get the name + email. That's a win — go to the cold email.)

### Decision maker (physician or manager)
> "Thanks for taking a second. I'll be quick. In January 2025 Medicare rolled out new **Advanced
> Primary Care Management** codes — they removed the minute-by-minute time-logging that made chronic
> care management not worth the hassle. Most practices haven't repriced it, and for a practice your
> size that's usually **six figures a year** left on the table.
>
> I do a flat-fee analysis called a **Practice Revenue Blueprint** — I take five numbers from you, no
> EHR access, and show you exactly what you're not capturing and three ways to capture it. It's
> backed by a guarantee: **if it doesn't find at least $50,000 in new annual revenue, you don't pay.**
>
> I'd love to send you a **free sample** so you can see precisely what you'd get. What's the best email?"

**Then book the next step:**
> "Once you've had a look, all I need is **15 minutes** to get your five numbers. Would **[day] morning
> or [day] afternoon** be better?"

Keep it conversational, not a pitch. You're a credentialed care-management expert offering useful news, not a vendor.

## Email-first approach (if you'd rather email before you call)

A clean way to warm up the call: email the sample first, then phone a few days later — "I sent you a
note with a sample analysis." It makes the call friendlier and you've already shown the work.

**Sourcing the emails (your NPI list has phones, not emails):**
- **Fastest free way:** open each practice's website → "Contact" page → grab the office email
  (often `info@` or `office@…`). ~1 minute each. For 15–20 targets this is quicker than it sounds.
- **At scale:** a data tool like Apollo can find emails in bulk, but coverage of small independent
  doctor's offices is hit-or-miss and it costs credits. Worth a test batch before committing.
- Add an **Email** column to your call list as you go.

**Deliverability — protect your sender reputation (important):**
- Send in **small daily batches (20–30 max)** from your Gmail, personalized, not one giant blast.
  Gmail caps ~500/day, but cold volume from a personal address gets flagged fast.
- For real volume later, use a **dedicated sending domain** + a cold-email tool (Apollo, Instantly).
  Not needed to land your first client — small batches are plenty.

**The 3-touch email sequence (per practice):**
1. **Day 1 — Initial.** The cold email above, sample attached.
2. **Day 4 — Short bump (reply to your own email):** *"Bumping this up — did the sample reach you? Happy
   to run your practice's number whenever you have 15 minutes. — Denise"*
3. **Day 8 — Breakup:** *"I'll stop here so I'm not cluttering your inbox. If capturing six figures of
   Medicare revenue is worth 15 minutes down the road, my door's open. — Denise"*
- No reply after touch 3 → move on. Pair any email thread with one phone call for the best hit rate.

## OBJECTION HANDLING

**"We already do CCM."**
> "Perfect — then you already know the time-tracking headache. The 2025 APCM codes pay a flat monthly
> amount with **no** time logging, and they usually pay **more**. The Blueprint shows you the difference
> in dollars. Worst case, you confirm you're optimized — and you don't pay if it doesn't find $50k."

**"We use a vendor for that."**
> "Good — and I'm not a vendor, I don't sell software or take referral fees. That's the point: the
> Blueprint shows you, independently, whether your vendor deal is leaving money on the table and what
> in-house or hybrid would net you instead. Practices are often surprised."

**"We're too busy / not interested."**
> "Completely fair, and that's exactly why I made it five numbers and a guarantee — no EHR access, 15
> minutes, and you don't pay unless it finds real money. Can I at least email you the free sample so
> it's there when you have a minute?"

**"Just send me information."**
> "Will do — what's the best email and who should I address it to? I'll send a one-page sample, not a
> brochure." *(Win. Get the email.)*

**"How much is it?"**
> "$1,500 flat — and it's guaranteed to find at least $50,000 or you don't pay, so it pays for itself
> 30-to-1 or it's free. For the first five practices I'm doing it at $750 for a testimonial."

**"Is this legit / who are you?"**
> "Fair question. I spent 15+ years in primary care practice transformation and ran a care-management
> company built on these exact codes. This is independent analysis — no software sale. Let me send the
> sample and you can judge the work yourself."

## Daily rhythm (fits one hour)
- 40 min dialing (~15–20 practices), goal: 2–3 real conversations + several emails sent.
- 10 min sending the sample + cold email to everyone you reached or left a voicemail for.
- 10 min logging and scheduling follow-ups.

One booked 15-minute intake is a great day. It only takes **one** to start.
